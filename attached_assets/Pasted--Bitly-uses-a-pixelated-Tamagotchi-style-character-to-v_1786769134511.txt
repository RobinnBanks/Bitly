“Bitly uses a pixelated Tamagotchi‑style character to visualize daily habits in a friendly, non‑clinical way.”

Updated behavior: login, logout, and navigation
Goal: When someone is logged in, the app behaves like a real product—no “About Us” clutter, clear session behavior, and Gmail remembered.

Remember Gmail:  
Label: Store email
Save the user’s email (e.g., Gmail) after login so the next time they visit, the email field can be pre‑filled or remembered in a “recent accounts” list.

Login page behavior (logged out):  
Label: Public view
Show:

Logo + tagline

“Log in” and “Sign up” buttons

“About Bitly” section

Simple explainer of the Tamagotchi buddy and glucose tracking

Option to choose account type: Parent / Teen / Kid (with “get your parent to make it” message for kids)

Login page behavior (logged in):  
Label: Authenticated view
Once logged in:

Hide “About Us”, “Log in”, and “Sign up” from the top navigation

Replace them with: “Home”, “Settings”, “Notifications”, “Log out”

Show a small banner: “Logged in as Parent” or “Logged in as Teen”

Logout behavior:  
Label: Session end
When the user logs out:

Clear session

Return to the public home page with “About Bitly”, “Log in”, and “Sign up” visible again

Do not delete their account—just end the session

Home page (logged‑in) — interactive parent/teen mode
Goal: Make the home page feel alive and useful the second they log in.

For logged‑in users, the Home page should show:

Glucose monitor status card:  
Label: Device status

Large, clear indicator: “Glucose monitor: Online” or “Offline”

For the demo, it should always show “Online” with a green dot

If online: show last sync time and a tiny icon of the Tamagotchi buddy smiling

If offline (future): show a gentle message like “We’re not seeing your monitor right now—check your connection.”

Tamagotchi buddy panel:  
Label: Buddy state

Show the pixelated character in its current mood (happy, hungry, tired, etc.)

Short text like: “Your buddy looks a little hungry. Have you eaten recently?”

Button: “Send reminder” (for parents) or “Set my own reminder” (for teens)

Daily goals card:  
Label: Goals

If Parent: “Set today’s goals for your child”

Example fields:

“Meals today” (e.g., 3)

“Water glasses” (e.g., 5)

“Check glucose” (e.g., 4 times)

If Teen: “Set your goals for today”

Same fields, but framed as self‑management

Show progress bars that fill as logs come in

Quick actions row:  
Label: Shortcuts

Buttons:

“Go to Settings”

“View Today’s Chart”

“Send a reminder”

“Check Tamagotchi buddy”

Notifications & Tamagotchi buddy interactions
Goal: Make the app feel like it’s talking through the buddy, not just sending boring alerts.

Parent notifications to kids:  
Label: Parent → kid
From the parent dashboard, they can:

Tap “Send reminder” when the buddy looks hungry or tired

Choose from preset messages like:

“Your Bitly buddy looks hungry—time for a snack?”

“Your buddy is low on energy—maybe check your glucose?”

“Water time! Your buddy wants a drink.”

These show up on the kid/teen’s phone as friendly notifications with the buddy’s face.

Automatic buddy‑based notifications (demo logic):  
Label: Auto prompts
For the demo, you can define simple rules like:

If no meal logged in 4 hours → send: “Your buddy hasn’t eaten in a while. Want to log a snack?”

If no water logged in 3 hours → send: “Your buddy is thirsty. Log some water?”

If glucose monitor is online but no reading logged → send: “Your buddy wants to check in. Log your latest reading?”

Notification center:  
Label: Inbox
Inside the app, have a “Notifications” tab where users can:

See past reminders

Mark them as “Done”

See how many times they responded to their buddy

Label: Entry points

From the logged‑in view: “Settings” in the top navigation and in the quick actions row.

Change email

Dark mode

Blue mode

Accessibility:

High contrast mode

Larger text

Dyslexia‑friendly font

Reduce animations (for motion sensitivity)

Notification preferences:

Turn buddy notifications on/off

Choose tone: “Gentle”, “Playful”, “Minimal”

Log out button

Accessibility behavior:  
Label: Visual safety

When high contrast mode is on, increase contrast between text and background.

When “reduce animations” is on, use fewer bounces and sparkles—keep the buddy calm.

Make all buttons large and easy to tap.

Buddies & images “online”
Goal: Make sure everything visual feels “live” and not placeholder.

Buddy status:  
Label: Online presence

Show a small “Online” indicator next to the buddy when the app is connected and synced.

If assets fail to load (future case), show a fallback buddy with a message: “We’re reloading your buddy—please wait.”

Image loading:  
Label: Asset handling

All character images, icons, and charts should appear instantly on the home page after login.

Use soft loading animations (like a pulsing pastel dot) instead of harsh spinners.
